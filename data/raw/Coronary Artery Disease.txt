Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
Coronary Artery Disease Definition

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is a condition in which the coronary arteries that supply oxygen-rich blood to the heart muscle become narrowed or blocked. This occurs due to plaque buildup, known as atherosclerosis, which reduces blood flow to the heart. CAD may remain asymptomatic for many years but can eventually lead to serious conditions such as heart attack, arrhythmia, or heart failure. It is also commonly referred to as coronary heart disease (CHD) or ischemic heart disease.

Coronary Artery Disease Types

There are two main types of coronary artery disease. Stable ischemic heart disease is a chronic condition in which the arteries gradually narrow over time, and symptoms are usually predictable. Acute coronary syndrome is a sudden condition caused by plaque rupture and blood clot formation, which can lead to a heart attack and requires immediate medical attention.

Coronary Artery Disease Symptoms

Many individuals with CAD may not experience symptoms for years. When symptoms occur, they commonly include stable angina, which is chest pain during physical activity or stress, and shortness of breath. In some cases, the first sign of CAD may be a heart attack.

Coronary Artery Disease Causes

The primary cause of CAD is atherosclerosis, which involves the buildup of plaque inside the arteries. This plaque is made up of cholesterol, calcium, fibrin, and other waste products. As plaque accumulates, it narrows the arteries and reduces oxygen supply to the heart, a condition known as myocardial ischemia.

Coronary Artery Disease Risk Factors

Risk factors for CAD include both non-modifiable and modifiable factors. Non-modifiable factors include age, particularly men over 45 and women over 55, and a family history of heart disease. Modifiable risk factors include smoking, unhealthy diet, physical inactivity, high blood pressure, abnormal cholesterol levels, diabetes, obesity, sleep apnea, chronic kidney disease, autoimmune diseases, and certain hormonal or reproductive factors.

Coronary Artery Disease Complications

If left untreated, CAD can lead to serious complications such as heart attack, arrhythmias, heart failure, cardiac arrest, and cardiogenic shock.

Coronary Artery Disease Diagnosis

Diagnosis involves a combination of clinical evaluation and risk assessment. Healthcare providers assess symptoms, medical history, family history, lifestyle factors, and blood pressure. Diagnostic tests may include blood tests, electrocardiogram (ECG), echocardiogram, stress testing, coronary calcium scan, CT coronary angiography, cardiac catheterization, heart MRI, and chest X-ray.

Coronary Artery Disease Treatment

Treatment aims to reduce symptoms, slow disease progression, and prevent complications. Lifestyle changes include quitting smoking, adopting a heart-healthy diet such as the Mediterranean diet, exercising regularly, and limiting alcohol intake. Managing risk factors such as diabetes, blood pressure, cholesterol levels, and body weight is also essential.

Medications may include antihypertensives to control blood pressure, statins to lower cholesterol, anti-anginal drugs such as nitroglycerin or ranolazine to relieve chest pain, and antiplatelet drugs to reduce the risk of blood clots. In more severe cases, procedures such as angioplasty with stent placement or coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG) may be required.

Coronary Artery Disease Recovery and Prognosis

CAD is a chronic condition that cannot be completely reversed, but its progression can be slowed with proper treatment and lifestyle changes. Recovery after procedures varies depending on the severity of the disease and the type of treatment. With appropriate management, many individuals can live long and functional lives, while untreated CAD significantly increases the risk of fatal complications.

Coronary Artery Disease Prevention

Prevention focuses on reducing risk factors. This includes avoiding tobacco use, maintaining a healthy diet, exercising regularly, controlling blood pressure and blood sugar, managing cholesterol levels, maintaining a healthy body weight, and treating conditions such as sleep apnea.
