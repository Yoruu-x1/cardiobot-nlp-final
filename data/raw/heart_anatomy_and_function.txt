Heart Definition

The heart is a fist-sized muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body. It is the main organ of the circulatory system and is made of muscle and tissue. The heart contains four chambers that temporarily hold blood before moving it, and electrical impulses control the heartbeat. The brain and nervous system help regulate its function.

Heart Function

The primary function of the heart is to pump blood throughout the body. Blood delivers oxygen and nutrients to cells and removes carbon dioxide and waste products. In addition, the heart helps regulate heart rate, maintain blood pressure, and coordinate circulation through interaction with other body systems.

The nervous system controls heart rate by sending signals that adjust the speed of the heartbeat based on activity or stress. The endocrine system releases hormones that influence blood vessel constriction and heart rate, including hormones from the thyroid gland.

Heart Structure Overview

The heart is made up of walls, chambers, valves, and blood vessels that work together to maintain circulation. It is divided into left and right sides by a muscular wall called the septum.

Heart Walls and Layers

The heart walls are muscular structures that contract and relax to pump blood. The wall has three main layers: the endocardium, which is the inner lining; the myocardium, which is the thick muscular layer responsible for contraction; and the epicardium, which is the outer protective layer. The heart is enclosed by the pericardium, a protective sac that contains fluid to reduce friction and protect the heart from surrounding organs.

Heart Chambers

The heart has four chambers: the right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, and left ventricle. The atria are the upper chambers, and the ventricles are the lower chambers.

The right atrium receives oxygen-poor blood from the body through the superior and inferior vena cava and sends it to the right ventricle. The right ventricle pumps this blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery. The left atrium receives oxygen-rich blood from the lungs through the pulmonary veins and sends it to the left ventricle. The left ventricle then pumps oxygenated blood to the rest of the body and is slightly larger because it performs more work.

Heart Valves

Heart valves control blood flow between chambers and prevent backflow. The atrioventricular valves include the tricuspid valve between the right atrium and right ventricle, and the mitral valve between the left atrium and left ventricle. The semilunar valves include the pulmonary valve, which allows blood to flow from the right ventricle to the pulmonary arteries, and the aortic valve, which allows blood to flow from the left ventricle to the aorta.

Blood Vessels of the Heart

The heart pumps blood through three main types of blood vessels. Arteries carry oxygen-rich blood from the heart to the body, except for the pulmonary arteries, which carry oxygen-poor blood to the lungs. Veins return oxygen-poor blood back to the heart. Capillaries are small vessels where the exchange of oxygen, nutrients, and waste products occurs.

Coronary Arteries

Coronary arteries supply blood to the heart muscle itself. The left coronary artery divides into the circumflex artery, which supplies the left atrium and parts of the left ventricle, and the left anterior descending artery (LAD), which supplies the front of the left ventricle and septum. The right coronary artery supplies blood to the right atrium, right ventricle, parts of the left ventricle, and the back of the septum.

Electrical Conduction System

The heart’s electrical conduction system controls the rhythm and rate of the heartbeat. Electrical signals begin in the sinoatrial (SA) node, which acts as the natural pacemaker. These signals travel to the atrioventricular (AV) node and then through the bundle of His, left and right bundle branches, and Purkinje fibers, causing the ventricles to contract and pump blood.

Heart Location, Size, and Appearance

The heart is located in the front of the chest, slightly behind and to the left of the sternum, between the lungs. The left lung is slightly smaller to accommodate the heart, and the rib cage provides protection. The heart is shaped like an upside-down pyramid and is approximately the size of a fist, weighing about 10 ounces in an average adult.

Heart Conditions and Disorders

Heart conditions are among the most common health problems. These include arrhythmia, cardiomyopathy, congestive heart failure, coronary artery disease, heart attack, heart valve disease, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and pericarditis. These conditions can affect the structure or function of the heart and may lead to serious complications.

Heart Symptoms

Common symptoms of heart-related conditions include chest pain, palpitations, dizziness, shortness of breath, fatigue, and swelling in the lower body.

Heart Diagnostic Tests

Several tests are used to evaluate heart health. These include blood pressure measurement, electrocardiogram (ECG), echocardiogram, chest X-ray, blood tests, cardiac catheterization, CT scan, heart MRI, and stress testing.

Heart Treatments

Treatment depends on the condition and severity. Options include medications to lower blood pressure or prevent clotting, lifestyle changes such as diet and exercise, medical devices like pacemakers, procedures such as catheter ablation or angioplasty, and surgeries including coronary artery bypass or valve replacement.
