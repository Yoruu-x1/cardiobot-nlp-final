Atherosclerosis
Atherosclerosis Definition

Atherosclerosis is a condition in which plaque builds up inside the walls of arteries. This plaque is made of fat, cholesterol, calcium, and other substances, which cause the arteries to become narrow and less flexible. As the condition progresses, blood flow to organs and tissues is reduced, increasing the risk of serious complications such as heart attack and stroke.

Atherosclerosis Symptoms

Symptoms of atherosclerosis often do not appear until an artery is significantly narrowed or blocked. The symptoms depend on the area affected. When the heart is involved, symptoms may include chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, dizziness, or pain in the back, arms, or neck. When arteries supplying the digestive system are affected, symptoms may include abdominal pain after eating, nausea, or weight loss. In the legs, it can cause muscle pain, skin changes, or slow-healing wounds. If the brain is affected, symptoms may include weakness on one side of the body, slurred speech, vision problems, or severe headache.

Atherosclerosis Causes

Atherosclerosis develops due to damage to the inner lining of the arteries, known as the endothelium. This damage can be caused by high cholesterol levels, especially LDL cholesterol, smoking, high blood pressure, diabetes, and other factors that trigger inflammation in the blood vessels.

Atherosclerosis Development Process

Atherosclerosis develops gradually over time. It begins with damage to the artery lining, which leads to inflammation. Cholesterol and other substances then accumulate, forming fatty streaks. Over time, these deposits grow into plaques that narrow the artery. In some cases, plaques can rupture and form blood clots, which may completely block blood flow.

Atherosclerosis Risk Factors

Several factors increase the risk of developing atherosclerosis. These include increasing age, family history of cardiovascular disease, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes, smoking, lack of physical activity, and an unhealthy diet.

Atherosclerosis Complications

Atherosclerosis can lead to serious health problems depending on the arteries affected. These include coronary artery disease, heart attack, stroke, peripheral artery disease, chronic kidney disease, and aneurysm.

Atherosclerosis Diagnosis

Diagnosis involves evaluating medical history, physical examination, and diagnostic tests. Doctors may check blood flow and listen for abnormal sounds in arteries. Blood tests can measure cholesterol levels, and imaging tests such as angiography, CT scan, ultrasound, or electrocardiogram (EKG) may be used to assess the condition of the arteries.

Atherosclerosis Treatment

Treatment aims to slow disease progression, relieve symptoms, and prevent complications. This includes lifestyle changes such as quitting smoking, following a healthy diet, and increasing physical activity. Medications may be used to control blood pressure, lower cholesterol, manage blood sugar, and prevent blood clots. In more severe cases, medical procedures such as angioplasty, stent placement, or bypass surgery may be required.

Atherosclerosis Prevention

Atherosclerosis can be prevented or delayed by maintaining a healthy lifestyle. This includes eating a balanced diet, exercising regularly, maintaining a healthy weight, avoiding tobacco, managing chronic conditions, and attending regular medical checkups.
