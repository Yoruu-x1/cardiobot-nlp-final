Heart Disease Overview

Heart disease describes a variety of conditions that affect the heart. The most common type is coronary artery disease (CAD), also known as coronary heart disease, which causes narrowing of the arteries and can lead to a heart attack. Heart disease can also affect the heart muscle, valves, or electrical system.

When the heart does not function properly, it cannot deliver enough blood, oxygen, and nutrients to the body. Lifestyle changes and medications can help maintain heart health and reduce risk. Heart disease is a leading cause of death worldwide.

Heart Disease Types

Heart disease includes several different conditions. These include coronary artery disease, which involves narrowing of blood vessels due to plaque buildup; arrhythmia, which refers to abnormal heart rhythm; heart valve disease, which affects blood flow through the heart; cardiomyopathy, which involves thickening or stiffening of the heart muscle; and heart failure, where the heart cannot pump enough blood.

Other types include congenital heart disease, which refers to structural defects present at birth, and pericardial disease, which involves inflammation or stiffness of the heart lining.

Heart Disease Symptoms

Common symptoms of heart disease include chest pain, shortness of breath, leg swelling, fatigue, and dizziness or fainting. Other symptoms may include palpitations, sweating, neck pain, heartburn or indigestion, nausea, trouble sleeping, reduced ability to exercise, and fever. Symptoms can vary depending on the specific condition.

Heart Disease Causes

Heart disease can develop due to several causes. These include blocked arteries, damage or scarring of the heart muscle, genetic factors, kidney disease, infections, thyroid disorders, autoimmune diseases, pregnancy-related complications, aging, certain medications such as chemotherapy, substance abuse, and high blood pressure.

Heart Disease Risk Factors

Risk factors increase the likelihood of developing heart disease. These include high blood pressure, high cholesterol, type 2 diabetes, obesity, smoking or tobacco use, physical inactivity, unhealthy diet, and a family history of heart disease.

Heart Disease Complications

Heart disease can lead to serious complications if not properly managed. These include heart attack, heart failure, cardiomyopathy, stroke, arrhythmia, cardiogenic shock, cardiac arrest, heart valve disease, pulmonary hypertension, and damage to organs such as the kidneys or liver.

Heart Disease Diagnosis

Diagnosis of heart disease involves a combination of medical evaluation and diagnostic testing. This includes physical examination, review of medical and family history, and tests such as electrocardiogram (ECG), echocardiogram, CT scan, MRI, blood tests, stress testing, cardiac catheterization, and ambulatory monitoring.

Heart Disease Treatment

Treatment depends on the specific type and severity of the condition. It may include lifestyle changes such as improving diet, increasing physical activity, and quitting smoking. Medications may be used to control blood pressure, cholesterol, or heart rhythm. In some cases, medical procedures or surgery, such as catheterization, ablation, or open-heart surgery, may be required. Cardiac rehabilitation programs can also support recovery and long-term health.

Heart Disease Recovery

Recovery time varies depending on the type of treatment. Minor procedures may require only a few days, while minimally invasive surgeries may take 2 to 4 weeks. Recovery from open-heart surgery may take 6 to 12 weeks.
