Blood Flow
Blood Flow Overview

Blood flow is essential for survival. The heart pumps oxygen-rich blood throughout the body through blood vessels. This circulation delivers oxygen and nutrients to tissues and removes carbon dioxide and waste products. Blood also carries immune cells, such as white blood cells, that help fight infection. The continuous movement of blood through the heart and blood vessels is known as the circulatory system.

Blood Flow Functions

Blood flow has two main functions, which are delivering oxygen and nutrients to organs and tissues and removing carbon dioxide and metabolic waste. In addition, blood helps transport immune cells that protect the body from infections.

Blood Flow Through the Heart Overview

The heart consists of four chambers: the right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, and left ventricle. These chambers work together to pump blood through the lungs and the rest of the body.

Blood Flow Through the Heart Process

Blood flows through the heart and body in a continuous cycle. Oxygen-poor blood enters the right atrium through the superior and inferior vena cava and moves into the right ventricle through the tricuspid valve. The right ventricle then pumps this blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery, where it becomes oxygenated.

Oxygen-rich blood returns to the left atrium through the pulmonary veins and flows into the left ventricle through the mitral valve. The left ventricle then pumps the oxygenated blood to the rest of the body through the aorta. From the aorta, blood travels through arteries and smaller arterioles until it reaches capillaries, where oxygen and nutrients are delivered and waste products are collected. The blood then returns to the heart through veins, completing the cycle.

Blood Flow Capacity of the Heart

The heart pumps a large volume of blood continuously to sustain circulation. On average, it pumps about 2,000 gallons of blood per day and beats around 100,000 times per day, which adds up to nearly 2.9 billion beats over a lifetime.

Blood Flow Disorders

Several conditions can affect normal blood flow in the body. These include atherosclerosis, which involves plaque buildup in arteries; aneurysms, which are caused by weakened artery walls; venous disease, which affects the function of veins; and arteriovenous fistula, which is an abnormal connection between arteries and veins. These conditions can reduce or disrupt normal circulation.

Improving Blood Flow

Blood flow can be improved through healthy lifestyle habits. These include maintaining a healthy weight, eating a heart-healthy diet, engaging in regular physical activity such as at least 150 minutes of exercise per week, limiting alcohol intake, managing stress, and avoiding smoking or tobacco use.
