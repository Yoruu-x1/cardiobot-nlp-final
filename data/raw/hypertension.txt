Hypertension Overview

Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is a condition in which the force of blood against the walls of the arteries is consistently too high. It is a major risk factor for heart disease, stroke, and kidney disease. Hypertension is often called a “silent killer” because it usually has no noticeable symptoms.

Hypertension Causes

Hypertension develops due to increased resistance in blood vessels or increased blood volume. Common contributing factors include genetic predisposition, high salt intake, obesity, physical inactivity, chronic stress, kidney disease, and hormonal disorders.

Hypertension Risk Factors

Risk factors that increase the likelihood of hypertension include increasing age, family history, overweight or obesity, unhealthy diet such as high sodium and low potassium intake, lack of physical activity, smoking, and excessive alcohol consumption.

Hypertension Symptoms

Hypertension often has no symptoms. However, in severe cases, symptoms may include headaches, dizziness, shortness of breath, and nosebleeds.

Hypertension Diagnosis

Hypertension is diagnosed by measuring blood pressure using a sphygmomanometer. Blood pressure readings are classified as normal when below 120/80 mmHg, elevated when between 120–129 mmHg systolic and less than 80 mmHg diastolic, and hypertension when equal to or above 130/80 mmHg. Diagnosis typically requires multiple readings over time.

Hypertension Complications

If left untreated, hypertension can lead to serious complications such as heart disease, stroke, kidney damage, vision loss, and damage to blood vessels.

Hypertension Treatment

Treatment focuses on lowering blood pressure through lifestyle changes and medication. Lifestyle changes include improving diet, increasing physical activity, managing weight, and reducing salt intake. Medications such as antihypertensive drugs may also be prescribed. Proper management significantly reduces the risk of complications.
